Circuits

Spielberg GP Circuit

The first circuit is the real Grand Prix layout at Spielberg in Austria, traced from OpenStreetMap at its true size and width. Names are kept generic for now, so the cars and the circuit carry made-up names.

  • 4,315 m a lap, with kerbs on the corners, run-off beside the track and a two-wide grid.
  • Laps are timed in three sectors, with your best lap and gaps on screen.
  • Cutting back across the line doesn't count as a lap.
  • A minimap in the corner shows where everyone is.

Garage

Four sports cars

Each car is built from real-world figures for its kind of car, so they drive differently: the rear-engined car rotates, the front-engined GT pushes wide, the light roadster carries speed through corners.

  • Flat-Six RS: a rear-engined flat-six coupe.
  • Berlinetta V8T: a mid-engined twin-turbo V8.
  • Grand Tourer V8: a front-engined V8 GT.
  • Featherweight S: a light supercharged roadster.
  • Each has its own weight, power curve, gearing, downforce, weight balance and grip.
  • Traction control and ABS help you on the throttle and brakes.

Tyres

Tyres that warm up

Every tyre has its own temperature. They start cold and grip less, warm up as you push, and lose grip again if you overdo it.

  • The outside tyres in a corner get the hottest: at Spielberg, which turns right more, the left-side tyres run warmer.
  • The HUD shows all four tyres (blue is cold, green is just right, red is too hot) and how much grip you have.
  • Sliding heats a tyre quickly; cruising lets it cool back toward the track temperature.

Weather

Air and track temperature matter

You set the air and track temperature before the race, and both change how the car behaves.

  • Hot air is thinner, so you get a little less power and downforce.
  • A cold track keeps the tyres cool and slippery; a hot one brings them up to temperature sooner, and can overheat them.
  • Time of day can be picked too. For now it only shifts the light; rain and changing weather come later.

Drivers

AI drivers

Fifteen named AI drivers, each with their own skill and consistency.

  • They follow the racing line, brake for the corners and ease off on cold tyres.
  • They steer round slower cars and get going again if they spin or go off.
  • In a test race of eight cars over several laps, every AI finished, with a best lap of about 1:54.
Online

Multiplayer

Online races, the Torque Shed way

Online works the same way as Torque Shed. One player picks Host online race, the others type the host's address into the join box and pick Connect.

  • The game uses UDP port 24690. The host's lobby shows exactly which address to give friends, and whether the router opened the port by itself (UPnP). If it didn't, forward UDP 24690 to the host's PC by hand.
  • The join box takes an address like 81.2.3.4, 81.2.3.4:24690, an IPv6 address or a host name.
  • Everyone drives their own car on their own PC, so there's no lag on your car. The host runs the AI cars and the timing.
  • If someone drops out mid-race, the AI takes their car home.
  • The first time you host, Windows asks about the firewall: tick both Private and Public networks.

Controller

Controller from the start

The game is fully playable with a pad, from the menus to the lobby to the race.

  • RT throttle, LT brake, left stick to steer. A and X also work as throttle and brake on pads without triggers.
  • Y rotates the camera, Back restarts and Start goes to the menu.
  • Menus: D-pad or stick to move, A to pick, B to go back, left and right to change a setting.
  • Keyboard: W A S D or the arrows, C for the camera, R to restart, Esc for the menu.
Race
1 Nov
Settings

Race settings

Laps from 1 to 50, up to 15 AI drivers, air temperature, track temperature and time of day. In an online race the host picks them.

Try it

  • Unzip the folder anywhere and double-click TopDownRacer.exe. Nothing to install. If Windows says it protected your PC, press More info, then Run anyway.
  • Run a single race on a cold track, then a hot one, and watch the tyre colours.
  • Try each car and feel the difference in the corners.
  • Host an online race and have a friend join with your address from the lobby.

Known limits

  • The AI is careful: about ten seconds a lap slower than the real cars would be.
  • You can't join a race that has already started. Wait in the lobby for the next one.
  • Weather, damage, fuel and pit stops, the safety car, upgrades, tuning and seasons aren't in yet.
