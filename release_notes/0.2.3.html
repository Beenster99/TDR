Top Down Racer

The game is now a real 3D racer seen from above. The cars are 3D models built from each car's real dimensions, with their own shapes, wings, lights and liveries. They sit on a 3D circuit with its real hills, kerbs, gravel traps, walls, grandstands full of people, pit garages and woods, all lit by the sun with real shadows. Cars
2 Nov
Graphics

Every car is a detailed 3D model

The cars are built in Blender from their real dimensions (length, width, wheelbase, track and wheel size) and a description of each one's shape: where the windscreen and roof sit, how wide the arches flare, the wing, the lights and the vents.

  • GT3 cars have their splitters, diffusers and swan-neck rear wings. The Porsches have round headlights on raised wings, the BMW has its big kidney grille, the AMG has its long bonnet and the Huracán has its roof scoop.
  • Wheels with spokes, brake discs and coloured calipers sit in real wheel arches. Through the glass you can see the driver's helmet and the roll cage.
  • The cars steer, their wheels spin, and the bodies roll in corners and dip under braking. Brake lights glow, and headlights come on after dark and in heavy rain.
  • Each model is split into parts (bumpers, splitter, wing, mirrors, wheels and so on) so that upgrades can change them later.
  • Damage shows on the car: scuffed and darkened panels where it was hit, bumpers and wings that sag and then fall off, a car that sits low on a puncture, and smoke from a damaged engine.
  • You can open every car in Blender: the .blend files are kept with the game's source.
Paint
2 Nov
Liveries

Liveries and race numbers

Paint has a clear-coat shine and a three-colour livery that lines up across every part of the car.

  • Each car has its own default livery, such as twin stripes on the Ferrari, M stripes on the BMW and a sash on the Porsche.
  • When several cars of the same model race, each extra one gets its own colour scheme so you can tell them apart.
  • Race numbers sit on a white panel on the roof.
  • The models are ready for painted liveries too, for when we add a livery editor.
Tracks
2 Nov
3D world

3D circuits with their real hills

Every circuit is built in 3D from its layout and the real elevation added in v0.2.1.

  • Asphalt shows a darker rubbered racing line, white edge lines and red and white kerbs on the corners.
  • Gravel traps sit on the outside of fast corners, with tarmac run-off elsewhere. Walls line the circuit, with tyre stacks where cars run wide.
  • There are grandstands full of people on the main straight and the biggest corners, pit garages with team trucks in the paddock behind, a start gantry, and woods and trees all around.
  • Hills show through real sun and shadow, extra shading on slopes, faint contour lines on the grass (every 2 m, darker every 10 m) and light lines across the road for every metre of height, which bunch up where it's steep.
Light
2 Nov
Weather

Sun, shadows and rain

A low sun makes long shadows that show the shape of the cars and the hills.

  • The sun moves with the time of day, turning golden in the evening. At night the scene goes dark and the headlights light the road.
  • Clouds soften the shadows. In the wet the road goes dark and shiny, the racing line dries first, and cars throw up spray.
  • The camera keeps the same controls and zoom. Fully zoomed in it now goes closer so you can see the cars, and the whole-track view still shows every car in 3D.

Try it

  • Unzip the folder and double-click TopDownRacer.exe, as before.
  • Zoom right in on the grid at Monza to see the cars, then zoom out over Spa to see the hills.
  • Try Mugello at 19:00 in heavy rain for the headlights and spray.

Known limits

  • The game now needs a graphics card with Vulkan or DirectX 12 (any gaming PC from the last eight years or so).
  • The cars are recognisable but not photo-exact, and their shapes will keep improving.
  • Starting a race takes a few seconds longer while the circuit is built.
