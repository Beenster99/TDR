Race data

Timing like a real pit wall

The lap is split into timing points about every 80 metres, and every car's time through each one is kept, so gaps are measured the way real timing does it.

  • Sector times for every car's last lap: purple for the fastest of anyone, green for that driver's own best.
  • Last lap, best lap and the average of the last 5 laps (the standing-start lap doesn't count), plus whether a driver is getting faster or slower.
  • Gap to the leader, interval to the car ahead (yellow when it's under a second), and laps down for lapped cars.
  • Tyres for every car: four little squares coloured from cold blue to hot red, the average temperature, and the compound.
  • Everything is saved when a race ends, so later tools can look back at whole races.

Strategy

When a gap opens for a stop

Each track's pit lane gives a time loss for a stop: the drive down the lane at the speed limit, plus the stop, minus the time the car would have spent on the track it skips (measured from its own laps).

  • The STOP NOW column shows the position each car would rejoin in if it pitted this lap, and marks it "clear" when it would come out in clean air.
  • The selected car panel spells it out: time lost, where you rejoin, the gap behind you now and the gap you need for a stop that costs no places.
  • When that gap opens for any car, the feed calls it: "Pit window: X can stop now and rejoin P5 in clear air".
  • Pit stops themselves arrive in wave 3; this is the analysis they'll use.

Weather

Weather radar and forecast

A radar view around the circuit shows cloud and rain drifting in on the wind, with the forecast next to it.

  • Rain shows on the radar well before it reaches the track: the outer ring is roughly 24 minutes away.
  • The forecast lists what's coming, when (as a window, like "6-8 min") and how sure it is. The further away, the less certain.
  • The feed warns at about 10, 5 and 2 minutes before a change, and when it arrives.
  • Needs changing weather turned on in the race settings; with fixed weather it shows the current conditions.

Feed

Other teams' radio and strategy

A running feed of what's happening, which you can filter with left and right on the D-pad.

  • Team radio from the AI drivers ("Ran wide, lost a bit of time", tyres going off, held up in traffic) and their strategy calls (push, manage, defend).
  • Timing calls: fastest laps, passes in the top ten, a car closing to within a second.
  • Tyres overheating, contacts between cars, weather changes, and pit windows opening.
  • Ready for race control: yellow flags, safety cars and penalties will appear here when wave 3 adds them.

Pit wall

The Pit wall screen

One full screen made for 4K and the controller: timing tower on the left, radar and forecast, the selected car and the feed on the right.

  • Open it with P or D-pad right. D-pad up and down picks a car, left and right filters the feed, B closes it.
  • The race keeps running underneath, so you can check it on a straight.
  • Online, everyone sees the same gaps and pit windows: the host works them out and sends them to the other players.
  • In wave 5 co-op this becomes the strategist's main screen.

Try it

  • Unzip the folder and double-click TopDownRacer.exe, as before.
  • Start a race with 15 AI cars and changing weather, then press P (or D-pad right) after a couple of laps.
  • Scroll to your car and watch the STOP NOW column as gaps open and close.

Known limits

  • Pit lane times come out long (35 to 55 seconds) because the tracks' speed-limit zones are still a rough default. They get fixed when pit lanes become drivable in wave 3.
  • The stop itself is a placeholder 8 seconds until pit work (tyres, fuel, repairs) exists.
  • On the first lap gaps are rough, as nobody has a lap time yet.
  • Players on 0.2.1 can still race with you, but they won't see the Pit wall.
