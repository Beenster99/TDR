Handling

Cars that are easier to handle

You found the car incredibly hard to handle. The physics is the same (tyre temperatures, load and grip still count), but you now get help to keep it pointing the right way, and the controls are calmer.

  • New stability assist, like ESC on a road car: when the back starts to slide it brakes one wheel to pull the car straight and eases the power.
  • It also adds counter-steer for you when the rear steps out.
  • At speed it stops the front wheels being turned far past the angle where they grip best, so the car no longer scrubs wide and then snaps round.
  • Controller steering is smoother: stick flicks are softened and small movements are finer. Steering lock reduces a bit more at speed, on keyboard and pad.
  • We tested beginner moves on all four cars (flooring it out of a turn, full lock, a quick left-right flick, lifting or braking mid-corner). With v0.1.0 the cars spun in 9 of 40; with full driving help, none of them spin.

Settings

Driving help setting

A new Driving help line on the main menu, under your car. It only affects your own car, online too.

  • Full (the default): stability assist, traction control and ABS.
  • Some: traction control and ABS, which is how v0.1.0 drove.
  • Off: the raw car, for when you want the challenge.
  • Full help costs a little pace, about 2 to 4 seconds a lap at Spielberg, in exchange for staying on the road.
Sound
1 Nov
Audio

The game has sound

Every sound is made by the game itself, so the download stays small.

  • Engines follow the revs and the throttle, with a burble on the overrun. The pitch matches each engine's cylinders, so the V8s, the flat-six and the four-cylinder roadster sound different.
  • You hear the three nearest rivals around you, panned left and right.
  • Tyres squeal as you slide, spin the wheels or brake hard, and there's a rough rumble on the grass.
  • A thump and crunch when you hit a barrier or another car, louder the harder you hit.
  • Beeps for the start lights and the green light, a chime at the end of each lap, and clicks in the menus.
View
1 Nov
Camera

Zoom right out to the whole track

You can now zoom smoothly from close behind your car all the way out until the whole track fills the screen.

  • Keyboard: Q and E (or minus and plus, or the mouse wheel) zoom out and in. Z or Tab jumps between close, middle and whole track.
  • Controller: the right stick zooms out and in. RB (or clicking the right stick) jumps between close, middle and whole track.
  • As you zoom out the camera moves to the middle of the track, so it all stays in view. The game remembers your zoom for the next race.
  • The whole-track view works out its size from your screen, so it fits at 1080p, 1440p or 4K. We checked it at 3840 x 2160.
  • When zoomed out, each car gets an arrow in its colour so you can still see it, and yours has a white ring.

Try it

  • Unzip the folder and double-click TopDownRacer.exe, as before.
  • Drive a lap on Full driving help, then try Some and Off.
  • Press Z (or RB) during a race to see the whole track, then zoom back in.
  • Turn the volume up and follow a rival closely.

Known limits

  • There's no volume setting yet; use Windows' volume mixer for now.
  • Rival engines guess their throttle from how fast they speed up, so they can sound a touch off.
  • The AI drives the raw car without the new assist, at the same pace as before.
